2 · Colour

Four inks, and what they make together

Riso printers use a few spot inks and get more colours by layering them. We do the same: four inks, two overprints, and 30% tints for big surfaces. Because everything comes from the same inks, it holds together on cream.

Rule 1

One dark block per screen

The hero card is Riso Ink. Everything else sits on paper and card, so your eye lands on the one number that matters.

Rule 2

Tints for areas, inks for accents

Large fills use the 30% tint. Full-strength ink is kept for icons, badges, progress and the + button.

Rule 3

No outlines, ever

Cards separate by fill and spacing. One soft shadow exists, for things that float (cards in the stack, the tab bar).

Contrast check

Calculated live from the hex codes using the WCAG formula. 4.5 passes for body text; 3 passes for large, bold text only.

5 · Mascot

Flapjack, aka Flappy

The character will be developed in ChatGPT from your references. Below are the style rules pulled from them, and two prompts ready to copy. Attach both reference images when you paste prompt 1.

What makes these work

  • 1The silhouette is the character. One simple, slightly irregular shape that you'd recognise in solid black.
  • 2One flat colour, no outlines. No gradients, shading or texture.
  • 3A tiny face, set slightly high. Dot eyes or white eyes glancing sideways, a small mouth, optional blush.
  • 4Emotion comes from eyes and squash. Sideways glances, sleepy lashes, a tilt, a stretch. No arms or legs needed.
  • 5Cute but a bit weird. Charming, never babyish.

Keep it consistent

Once you have a version you love, keep working in the same ChatGPT chat and say "same character, same proportions" each time. New chats drift.

Get clean vectors

ChatGPT gives you images, not true vectors. To turn the final into SVG, use Illustrator's Image Trace or vectorizer.ai. Simple flat shapes trace cleanly.

Then bring it back

Send me the final expressions and I'll drop them into the dashed Flappy spots in the screens above.

6 · Where it could go

Couples, plus cards

Couples apps (Honeydue, Splitwise) handle sharing but not card rewards. Card apps (HeyMax's Card Maximiser) track caps and min spends but aren't a household ledger. Flapspenditure sits in the overlap, which happens to be the purple.

"Which card?" for both of you

Suggest the best card across both partners' wallets: who's short of a min spend, and who's near a cap.

Bill radar

Statement and due dates per card, the amount, and which account pays. One "bills this week" strip on Home.

Monthly rewards recap

Miles or cashback earned, plus spends that earned nothing (the connector can already list these).

Things to weigh

  • Card rules change often, so pushing optimisation further means someone keeps caps and min spends up to date.
  • Manual entry is the bottleneck for other couples. HeyMax auto-tracks linked Visa cards.
  • This is from a quick search, not a full market scan. Worth a proper look before treating it as a product.

Decisions for you two

  1. Flappy: run prompt 1 in ChatGPT, pick a concept, then run prompt 2 for the full sheet.
  2. Hero card colour: Riso Ink (calm, shown here) or Riso Blue (louder)?
  3. Next screens: a Card bonus deep-dive, the calendar view, or Recurring?
  4. Jeremy: owner grouping and card progress use data the app already has. Bill dates, splits and "which card?" would be new.
