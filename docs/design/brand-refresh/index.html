Why A

Bagel Fat One is closest to your Extra Chunk reference, and Modak is the nearest free match to Mocha Mocha's bubble. For a ledger, the body font also needs "tabular figures" (every digit the same width) so amounts line up in columns. The specs on each card above test this live in your browser. If a font fails, we keep it for text and set amounts in the chunky face, which already reads as numbers. Pairing B is gentler and might suit Jeremy if A feels too loud.

4 · Mascot

Three ways to be flappy

Each concept takes "flap" differently. All are simple flat shapes with no outlines, so they work at 24px in the tab bar and at full screen.

My pick

A · Wings

A round little piggy-bank monster with stubby wings and a coin slot on its head. Flaps when it's happy, droops when you overspend.

👍 The wings give it a clear silhouette, and the coin slot says "money" without words.👎 It's the most "cute" of the three, so it may read young.

B · Floppy ears

A tall bean with long ears that flop and swing. Ears up for good news, ears flat for bad.

👍 Ears are very expressive and easy to animate.👎 It reads closer to a bunny than a monster.

C · Wallet flap

The monster is a coin purse. Its flap is its lid, which pops open to "eat" a receipt when you log one.

👍 The most ownable idea, since it's literally a wallet.👎 It's boxier, so less huggable.

Concept A, four moods

Each mood comes with a line of copy in the mascot's voice.

Happy

"On track! Keep flapping."

Default state. Under budget and on pace.

Over budget

"Uh oh, Eating out is S$48 over."

Wings droop, sweat drops, wobbly mouth. Worried, not angry.

Goal hit

"Min spend smashed! Miles incoming."

Wings up, confetti, drops a coin in its own slot.

No-spend day

"Zero spent today. Nap earned."

A bonus mood, for days with nothing logged.

Names

FlapjackYour favourite. "Flap" plus a pancake, which fits concept A's round, flat body. Shortens to "Jack".
FlipShort and bouncy. A coin flip, and it fits in a tiny badge.
PennyPairs with the coin slot. Reads softer and more grown-up.
MoShort for monster and for moolah. Two letters, friendly in copy: "Mo says…"
ClaspOnly works for concept C, the wallet.

6 · Where it could go

Couples plus cards

Most trackers are built for one person. Couples apps (Honeydue, Splitwise) handle sharing but not card rewards. Card apps (HeyMax's Card Maximiser) track caps and min spends but aren't a household ledger. Flapspenditure sits where the two overlap.

"Which card?" for both of you

When logging (or before paying), suggest the best card from either partner's wallet: who's short of a min spend and who's near a cap. Card apps only see one person's cards.

Bill radar

Statement and due dates per card, the amount, and which account pays it. One "bills this week" strip on Home.

Monthly rewards recap

Miles or cashback earned, plus spends that earned nothing. The connector can already list those. Flapjack presents it.

Things to weigh

  • Card rules change often. Caps, min spends and bonus categories shift every few months, so someone has to keep them current. That's the real cost of pushing this further.
  • Manual entry is the bottleneck. HeyMax tracks linked Visa cards automatically. Here, everything is logged by hand or through Claude. That's fine for you two, but a hurdle for other couples.
  • From a quick search, not a full market scan: the couples + card-optimisation combo looks uncommon. It's worth a proper look before treating it as a product idea.

Decisions for you two

  1. Mascot: A (wings), B (ears) or C (wallet flap)? And keep the name Flapjack?
  2. Type: pairing A (loud) or B (gentler)?
  3. Palette trims: happy for Taffy and Riviera to become the Cheryl and Jeremy colours?
  4. Next screens to mock: a Card bonus deep-dive, the calendar view, or Recurring?
  5. Jeremy: anything here that's a pain to build? The owner grouping and card progress use data the app already has. Bill dates, splits and "which card?" would be new.
